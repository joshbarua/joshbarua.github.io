Hey there! I'm a research fellow at the Cambridge-Boston Alignment Initiative, working with Aaron Mueller on interpretability. Before that, I was a predoctoral researcher at UT Austin with Kyle Mahowald. And before that, I was an undergrad at UC Berkeley 🐻, where I was fortunate to get my start in research with Alane Suhr as part of the Berkeley NLP Group. I've also spent great summers interning at CMU LTI with Graham Neubig and the MIT Lincoln Lab.

Research interests

Current local minima
Reasoning in LLMs
Recently, I've been exploring the underlying principles of reasoning in LLMs: to what extent it generalizes, how we can control it, and what the role of human-like reasoning is in model computation.
Steepest directions for gradient updates
Human–model collaboration & oversight
How can humans learn from, collaborate with, and oversee "superhuman" models? Can we design guardrails that better scale with model capabilities?

Science of language models
I am interested in understanding the structure of representations and the kinds of solutions neural networks learn. Can we find simple explanations in small models and controlled testbeds that reliably predict more complex phenomena?

In addition to research, I spent three fun semesters teaching the data structures & algos class (CS 61B) at Berkeley. In my free time, I enjoy climbing rocks, throwing discs, and sending funny cat videos to Angela.
